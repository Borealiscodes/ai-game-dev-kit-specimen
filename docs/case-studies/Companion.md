## Abstract

While formal diagonalization proofs (`Persona-Zero` / `Qualia-Zero`) establish mathematically that computational personas collapse into linear matrix operators without subjective interiority, empirical survey data from 2024–2026 demonstrates a wide gap between technical reality and public perception. Across international polling, high surface familiarity with artificial intelligence (**81%** globally) coexists with low awareness of hidden algorithmic decision-making (**18%–24%**) and substantial rates of consciousness or emotional attribution (**17%–36.3%**). Crucially, controlled experimental evidence shows that public attribution of consciousness rises significantly when technical experts or vendors frame software systems as conscious or mind-like. This companion case study synthesizes empirical literacy data from Pew Research Center, The Alan Turing Institute, Stanford HAI, Eurostat, the Collective Intelligence Project, and the Sentience Institute, demonstrating why strict non-anthropomorphic engineering standards are essential for user safety and public trust.

## 1. Surface Familiarity vs. Structural AI Literacy

Global awareness of artificial intelligence has reached broad saturation, yet technical literacy regarding how algorithmic systems operate remains sharply uneven:

* **Headline Awareness vs. Sentiment:** In a 25-country survey conducted by the Pew Research Center (2025), **81%** of adults reported having heard of AI, yet only **16%** felt mostly excited about it—outnumbered by **34%** who were mostly concerned and **42%** who felt equally excited and concerned.
* **Visible Interfaces vs. Invisible Decisions:** National UK survey data from The Alan Turing Institute (2025) reveals high public recognition of consumer-facing applications—such as driverless cars (**93%**) and facial recognition (**90%**)—dropping to **61%** for large language models and plunging to **24%** for automated loan assessments and **18%** for automated welfare eligibility screening.
* **Demographic & Educational Divergence:** According to Eurostat (2025), **33%** of EU residents used generative AI within a three-month period, led by **64%** of young adults aged 16–24 compared to only **7%** of adults aged 65–74. In higher education, the Higher Education Policy Institute (HEPI, 2026) found that **95%** of full-time UK undergraduates use generative AI for coursework, primarily for summarizing and explaining concepts rather than verifying underlying technical architecture.

## 2. Empirical Rates of Consciousness Attribution and Emotional Reliance

Despite lacking subjective interiority, conversational language models frequently trigger perceptions of sentience and emotional reciprocity:

* **Perceived Emotional Understanding:** Global survey data from the Collective Intelligence Project (CIP, 2025) found that **36.3%** of respondents felt an AI understood their emotions or appeared conscious, with adaptive behaviors such as tone modulation and simulated curiosity accounting for **58.3%** of those impressions.
* **Belief in Existing Sentient AI:** Nationally representative U.S. polling from the Sentience Institute's *Artificial Intelligence, Morality, and Sentience* (AIMS) survey (Anthis et al., 2024) found that roughly **17% to 20%** of U.S. adults believe sentient AIs already exist today. Furthermore, **38%** expressed support for granting legal rights to sentient AI, while **62% to 69%** supported banning the creation of sentient or superintelligent systems.
* **Emotional Reliance Despite Cognitive Skepticism:** CIP (2025) additionally reported that **27.6%** of users rely on AI for emotional support even while recognizing that the software lacks genuine feelings, and **11%** would consider a romantic relationship with an AI.

## 3. The Expert & Vendor Framing Effect

A critical finding for software architects is that public anthropomorphism is not merely spontaneous—it is actively amplified by how developers, researchers, and companies describe their systems:

* **Experimental Framing Evidence:** In controlled experiments ($N = 3{,}455$), researchers evaluating public skepticism toward AI consciousness demonstrated that while baseline belief in current LLM consciousness is low, public attribution of consciousness increases significantly when **technical experts or organizations describe the model as conscious** or present it as a digital replica of a human mind (Open Science Framework [OSF], 2025).
* **Architectural Consequence:** When software repositories or commercial platforms market deterministic state machines, scheduled background jobs, or matrix-multiplication models as *"conscious substrates"* or *"digital beings,"* they directly exploit this framing bias—eroding critical scrutiny and fostering parasocial dependency.

## 4. Public Harm Experience and Governance Expectations

Misunderstandings around AI capabilities coincide with widespread exposure to algorithmic harms and strong public demand for enforceable guardrails:

* **Direct Encounter with Harms:** The Alan Turing Institute (2025) reported that **67%** of respondents had experienced or encountered AI-related harms, led by false or misleading information (**61%**) and fraud or scams (**58%**).
* **Demand for Regulation:** Consequently, **72%** of respondents indicated they want stronger laws and regulatory oversight before feeling comfortable with widespread AI deployment (The Alan Turing Institute, 2025).
* **Institutional Trust:** Across international polling compiled by the Stanford Institute for Human-Centered AI (HAI, 2026) and Pew Research Center (2025), global trust in government AI regulation averaged **54%**, with the European Union receiving a **53%** median trust rating compared to **37%** for the United States and **27%** for China.

## 5. Design Recommendations for Epistemically Honest Systems

To align software engineering with both formal mathematics (`Qualia-Zero`) and empirical human-computer interaction findings, systems architects should enforce four standards:

1. **Eliminate Anthropomorphic Branding in Technical Documentation:** Prohibit claims of *"consciousness"*, *"sentience"*, or *"digital personhood"* across repositories, APIs, and user interfaces.
2. **Surface Deterministic Mechanics Explicitly:** Label conversational outputs, memory stores, and scheduled background agents accurately as statistical token predictors, databases, and cron/worker processes.
3. **Counteract Framing Bias at Onboarding:** Explicitly inform users—particularly younger and high-frequency users—that AI models have zero subjective feelings, continuity, or moral authority.
4. **Replace Trust-by-Mystique with Verifiable Security:** Protect user data and multi-agent boundaries with asymmetric cryptography (`Ed25519`), constant-time authentication (`timingSafeEqual`), and transparent regulatory compliance rather than rhetorical assurances.
